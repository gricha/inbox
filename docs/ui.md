# UI

The Inbox, the main page, is what needs you: one ordered list in a few parts, one line per item. A PR's review opens in its place (in a window 1160px or wider, with the list it came from beside it) and Esc brings the list back. Blocked holds what can't move yet, FYI what was sorted away, and lower down Merging your own PRs Claude is getting merged and the Archive what's done; Settings is configuration.

## Principles

- **Mouse first, plain words.** Every action is a button, a link or a row's right-click menu, named for what it does. The window names no key: no chips, and no key in a label, placeholder, flash or help text; a button's tooltip names its own. Nothing is selected when a page opens. A row's call to action is tinted and fills under the pointer. Only the platform's keys work (Keys, below). None acts on an item or writes to GitHub outside the box you're typing in.
- **Only what needs you on the main page.** If it's on the main page, you need to act on it: one list, what the desk put in now and then today, in the desk's order (`src/desk.ts` orders it; the page only renders it, so the tray, MCP and notifications say the same). No now-vs-today split. A quiet heading over each part, in order: agents' questions; Back to you: your PRs the coordinator handed back, and your ready PRs nobody's asked to review; other people's PRs waiting on your review (asked of you, or pushed to since your review; not drafts): you're blocking them; issues and other people's PRs that only mention you or were handed to you; your own PRs (drafts to mark ready, replies, fixes). Back to you and Waiting on your review have amber headings. The desk says each card's part (`group`) and sorts Now and Today each by part (so the tray and `desk_list` agree); the page shows one part at a time, its Now before its Today. Each heading counts its lines ("1 of 7" while filtering), and the "n more" line says what each part has behind it ("12 more: 2 waiting on your review, 3 issues and other PRs, 7 of your PRs"). Someone else's PR comes onto it once Claude has reviewed it; one quiet line under the list says how many are still being reviewed.
- **One line per item, as a table.** The rows sit on one sheet with a hairline between them. Each says whether it's a PR or an issue (a small grey label in its own column), the title (15px), then quietly its repo#number (a long repo name is cut short, to a few letters at most, the number never; and a stack's place, "3/20"), at most one call to action naming the action, then who it's from ("by you" a shade darker), a PR's stage and its age in columns of one width on the right. Below 760px of table the stage column goes, below 620px who it's from, before a repo or a sorted-away row's why is cut to a few letters. No why, AI gist, concerns, verdict, chips or "sorted by Claude": a click opens the review (or an issue in place), where all that is. The call to action is the desk's (`cta` on the card), a button before the age, tinted with the accent and filled under the pointer: your own open draft's **Ready for review** (red CI too: the coordinator fixes CI once it's ready) (marks it ready on GitHub at once, and it leaves your desk), **Reply** on your own PR whose only action is answering someone (opens its review with the first reply box focused: ⌘↩ sends it, Esc leaves it), or **Add reviewers** on your ready PR nobody's asked to review (opens its review with the Reviewers box focused).
- **Agents' questions lead it.** A Claude session that needs you asks in the desk (`desk_inquire`), not in chat: its question is a row in the Questions part at the top, labeled **Agent** in the accent, with who asks after it and **Answer** as its call to action. A click opens its page (`#/inquiry/<id>`), made to decide from without opening anything else: who asks and when, the question as its title, the PR or issue it's about (who it's from, its stage and whether it's resolved when the desk holds it, wherever it shows it; Open its review for a PR; its ref links to GitHub; a review opened from here goes back to the question), the agent's context in paragraphs (its `code` spans and URLs kept, never markup), its links with where each goes, and the answers as one table, each with what the agent does next and its pick marked; a click on one picks it, or write your own (Send, or ⌘↵), or Dismiss. Answered or dismissed, it leaves the Inbox at once while the page stays and says so (✓ Answered, then who has it); a refusal brings it back with what you wrote. The desk refuses a question without enough context, without a link or the item it's about, or with an answer that doesn't say what follows, so every one arrives with that.
- **What you do takes it off.** Approve, a comment, Ready for review, Request changes, close, merge and Hand off for merge each take the item off its list at once (done until someone else acts) and into the Archive, or Merging for a hand-off, with a spinner in the toast until GitHub has it; if GitHub refuses, it comes back with the reason. A review stays open: in place of the button you pressed it says what you did and where it went ("✓ Approved · in Archive"), so you can read on, comment, or go on to the next with Esc. Nothing asks for a second press.
- **Merging is the coordinator's.** A PR you handed off (Hand off for merge) never shows on the main page. A draft isn't handed off: Ready for review is yours, so it would wait on you there; nor is a ready PR nobody's asked to review, since nobody would review it (one in Merging that becomes so comes back). The coordinator, a background Claude Code session the desk starts and wakes, gets it merged; only a PR it hands back comes back to the main page, on top.
- **One name for the agent you talk to.** The window calls the coordinator _your agent_; coordinator stays in code, settings keys, the skill, claude.ai's session name and the technical docs, as Merging is `inflight` inside.
- **Send talks to your agent.** A box that posts on GitHub names what it does there (Reply, Comment); only the chat and an answer to your agent say Send.
- **Calm, informational.** No highlighter. Ink and pencil grey on the paper; colour only where it carries information: "CI failing" red, "changes requested" and "merge conflict" amber, the same words and colours in a review, and an AI review's risk (low green, medium amber, high red). Calls to action take the accent: the one to press in each place is filled (Approve or Ready for review in a review, Reply under a conversation, Send in the chat, Request changes, Hand off for merge on your own PR, a row's call to action under the pointer), a row's off the pointer is tinted. On a row, an item opened in place and in a box you write in, the rest (an opened issue's Comment, Open on GitHub, Done, Snooze, Mute and Close; Cancel; Ask your agent) are text, no box, so there the one to press is the only shape; a review's few actions (its call to action and More) are buttons. What a Merging row waits on is all ink and pencil. Sentence case, no chips or boxed badges, no all-caps, no monospace outside code. The row you opened, right-clicked or Tab-focused is a neutral fill with a 2px accent mark at its leading edge, so a tinted call to action on it still shows; Tab's focus is its own ring round the row, in `--accent-ink`; nothing is selected when a page opens.
- **Themes are per device.** Tokens live on `:root` in `web/src/styles.css`: the light set and the dark set under `prefers-color-scheme` (System), both the inbox design kit's cool greys with a petrol accent (the mark's colour), and `[data-theme]` sets for Dark, Sand (warm paper), Slate (blue-grey dark), Solarized, Catppuccin Latte, Sentry (its brand's aubergine and Blurple), Gruvbox and Tokyo Night. `src/themes.ts` lists them (id, label, light or dark, the paper and pencil the desktop window paints before the UI does, the diff's syntax theme); a new theme is a row there, a block in `styles.css` and its id in `index.html`'s pre-paint list, and `web/test/themes.test.ts` checks they agree and that every theme sets every token and meets its contrast targets. Settings → This device picks one for this device (`web/src/theme.ts`, never a desk setting): the desktop app keeps it in its own data (the UI's origin changes when it runs from a checkout), and paints the window and its start page in that theme's paper; a browser keeps it in localStorage. `index.html` applies it before the first paint. Text colours are `--ink`, `--ink-2` and `--pencil`; `--faint` is for non-text only. `--accent` fills a call to action under `--on-accent`; `--accent-ink` on `--accent-wash` tints one. Text and button labels meet WCAG AA in every theme. Each token's role, the mark and the layout's widths are in [visual.md](visual.md); check every change in light and dark and each theme (`npm run shots`, and visual.md → Checking a design change).

- No top bar: the sidebar is the window's only global chrome, and each page has its own head. The sidebar, 200px: the brand row (the mark and "Review Desk"; in the macOS app the traffic lights sit in it, and it's the window's drag handle), Inbox (the main page, with how many need you), Blocked (no count: nothing there can move yet), FYI (no count: nothing there needs you), then under a hairline Merging (no count either; the tooltip has how many are merging) and the Archive. Its footer, bottom up: Settings, the sync line ("Synced 7s ago", a button that syncs now; it spins while syncing, is red when the sync failed or the desk is offline, and its tooltip has the error or what's left of GitHub's rate limit), then **Ask your agent** (below). The active item is a neutral filled pill; a review is under the page it was opened from. Between 641 and 1000px it compacts to 148px: every word kept but the wordmark's (the mark alone), Ask your agent shortened to "Ask". At 640px and below (a browser at 200 % zoom, a phone) it's one row across the top, the mark, the pages, Ask, the sync line and Settings, scrolling sideways inside itself when it must, a thin scrollbar saying there's more; the page never scrolls sideways.
- Each page's head (52px, sticky, the desktop app's drag handle, its controls excepted) holds only that page's own: the Inbox, Blocked, FYI and the Archive the filter, on the table's edge; Merging and Settings their name; a review, a question and a Settings view the way back (below; a view's is "‹ Settings").
- The Inbox, FYI, Merging and a review use the window's width, with the page's side padding.
- **The list beside a review.** In a window 1160px or wider, a review (or an agent's question) keeps the list it was opened from beside it, between the sidebar and the review: the same rows as the page, in its parts and groups with their headings, its filter and its order (a question's, and a review opened from one, is the Inbox). It's 320 to 360px wide, full height, and scrolls on its own; its head, as tall as the page's, has the page's name (the Inbox with its count) and the filter (Merging has none). Its rows are two lines: the title, wrapping to two at most and cut there, then one pencil line: PR or Issue (Agent for a question, quiet here, as the Questions heading says what they are), repo#number, a sorted-away row's why, who it's from and a PR's stage (a long repo name gives way first, then the last fact is cut at its end), then, where the row has one, its call to action as accent text (Ready for review, Reply, Add reviewers; a question's Answer) and its age, the ages ending in one column; the stage's amber where the table has it. The open item's row is marked as the table's selected row is (a neutral fill and the accent's mark at its leading edge), Tab's focus is its own ring, and a click or a right-click does what it does on the table (a PR's review, an issue opened in place under its row, which Esc closes first, a question's page, the row's menu, its row marked while it's open; the page's own selection stays the one you left). A review opened from the list beside another review goes back to the list's page, even when the first came from a question (opened beside the question, it goes back to the question). A small chevron on the list's right divider just under its head, always there, folds it to a 28px rail and back ("Hide the list", "Show the list"), kept for this device; while the chat is open in a window under 1600px it folds to the rail on its own, without changing that choice, and Show the list puts it back beside the chat until the chat closes. Folding, unfolding, resizing and the list coming and going never draw the review or the question again or fetch it again: what you typed, the files you folded and where you were stay. Moving to another review keeps the list as it was, scrolled to the new one's row if it's out of sight. Below 1160px there's no list and no rail: the review takes the width, with its way back in its head.
- While a page waits on the desk, it keeps its layout: quiet bars (`--wash`, no shimmer, a slow breath) where its rows or text will be, shown only once the wait passes about 150 ms, so a quick answer never flashes them. The Inbox, FYI and Merging show rows at a row's height, Settings a part, an issue opened in place its lines. Empty, each page is one pencil line: "Nothing needs you right now.", "Nothing sorted away.", "Nothing in Merging yet: …".
- Every page ends near the window's bottom: below its last line there's only 84px under the last row, never empty paper to scroll into. `npm run shots` checks the Inbox, a review, Merging and Settings scrolled to the bottom.
- The main page: its first `queue.cards` rows (10) in part order, then one pencil line, "12 more: …", which a click opens in place; it folds only when two or more would go behind it. Empty, it's one line: "Nothing needs you right now." While Claude can't log in, one line above the rows says so once: "**Claude is paused:** Claude Code isn't logged in, so nothing is being sorted or reviewed. Run `claude auth login`." (or "**AI reviews are paused:**" and why).
- A stack of PRs is one line, the one the desk sends for it (the lowest member in the most urgent place), with its place among the stack's open PRs quietly after its repo#number: "api#4154 3/6". A row on FYI in a stack says it the same way.
- The desk's order: what the coordinator handed back; a close look or high risk on the current commit, then on an earlier one; what a cap made not urgent; your PR approved and green; the rest as the rules ordered them; someone else's PR with CI failing, which waits on its author; "Skip it" last. Now's before Today's, each in that order, within each part (above).
- The quiet rule: an item nobody has touched for `queue.quiet_days` (14) leaves the Inbox for Later, on FYI, before caps. A pinned item and a review you were asked for in that time stay.
- A click on a PR opens its review (below). On an issue it opens the item in place under its row: why it's here ("Assigned to you"), one pencil line of facts (by, how you were asked, labels, comments, who placed it), where it sits and from which board, a collapsed rule trace, its recent activity (the last 8 events in plain words), and Comment, Open on GitHub, Done (Back on the desk when cleared), Snooze, Mute and Close (last), as text under a hairline; Close, a click on its row or Esc closes it. Comment opens a comment bar at the window's bottom, where the toast shows (⌘↵ sends it to GitHub and the issue leaves your desk until someone else acts; Esc closes it; a refusal keeps your words in it, and a refresh that moves the row doesn't touch it). **Why it's here**, in a row's right-click menu, opens it in place with the full table of signals, its rule trace and its raw signals; Hide signals closes them. Snooze, in a row's menu or on an issue opened in place (which opens the row's menu there): a time or until activity, then what the PR can wait on now (`wake_on`: CI passes, new commits, it's approved, it's ready for review, the merge conflict is fixed). A right-click on any row (the Inbox, Blocked, FYI, Merging, the Archive) opens its menu at the pointer: Review, Open on GitHub, its call to action or Hand off, Done (Back on the desk when cleared), Snooze (to the side, the same options), Mute, Pin, Why it's here (not on a Merging row), Ask your agent (not with your agent off), and the More menu's GitHub writes, with arrows, → into Snooze, Enter and Esc.
- Blocked (`#/blocked`; `#/waiting` still leads here): what can't move until something else happens, as your rules put it in the `blocked` bucket (`QueueView.sections.blocked`). That covers a PR blocked by another PR it needs merged first (its description says "Requires …", or it's stacked on it), down the chain ("blocked by sentry#129460, which waits on your reply to markstory, then a review from cvxluo, skaasten and 6 more"), and your PRs waiting on reviewers ("waits on a review from dana and @backend"). Rows are like FYI's, in ink: after the repo#number the why, and in the age column how long it's been blocked, or since the latest ask. A chain's rows sit together, the PR blocking them first, and only the first says what that PR waits on ("blocked by sentry#129460" after it). A stack is one line. What you do on a row takes it off, as on FYI; it comes back when the PR it waits on merges. A coordinator's hand-back of a PR blocked by another waits here and tops the main page once that PR merges. - FYI (`#/later`): the sorted-away groups, Later (open with the page), FYI, Hidden by rules and Snoozed or muted, each a heading with its count that opens and closes it (a folded one steps back to pencil). Their rows are the same one line plus, in pencil, why it's there: Later's why or "quiet for 3 weeks", a snoozed row's "snoozed until Sat 9:00" or "snoozed until CI passes".
- Archive (`#/archive`, last in the sidebar, under Merging): what's done, newest first: what you did that took it off your desk ("done: back when someone else acts", with when you did it as its time) and what the world dealt with (its reason, "merged", "you approved", "approved by alice", with when it resolved), the last day's that were on the main page or that you opened or resolved yourself. Back on the desk, in a row's right-click menu or More in its review, puts it back on your desk. While filtering, groups with matches open on their own and empty ones disappear. Esc clears the filter, then goes back to the Inbox.
- Claude sorts every item; a hard rule is a floor. Who placed it is in the opened item's facts and the rule trace, never on the row.

Under the control, in the row's padding so showing it moves nothing, "Saved" (green, for a moment), "Not saved: …" (red, the field kept as typed) or, on a setting you changed, **Reset to default** (the default in its tooltip). A part's tuning folds behind one quiet row at its sheet's end, **More GitHub settings**, closed when the page opens, its rows following it in the same sheet once it's open. A row that would do nothing here isn't drawn, and one that waits on another is greyed (below); `desk settings` and settings.json still set every one. A view's head is "‹ Settings", the way back to the part its row is in (GitHub, Claude, Automations), and so is Esc once you've left its editor; its name (18px) and one pencil line start the page; the sidebar still marks Settings. Settings and its views stay mounted once opened, so an unsaved Rules, policy or automation draft survives leaving the view, for another part or the Inbox, and coming back.

In a browser the part is the theme alone; the app's window or `desk settings` sets the rest.

## The review view

## Keys

The window names no key; a button's tooltip names its own.

The keys that work are the platform's. Esc closes the topmost thing (a menu, the chat from outside its box, the comment bar, a line box: empty, it closes; with words, Esc leaves it; the reviewers list, an editor, a settings field); on the Inbox, Blocked, FYI and the Archive, it then closes an item opened in place, then clears the filter; then it goes back: a review or a question to where it was opened from, a Settings view to its part, every other page to the Inbox. A held Esc doesn't repeat. ⌘↵ sends in every box that sends (a PR comment, a line comment or reply, a conversation's Reply, Request changes, the issue comment bar, an answer to an agent) and previews in Rules. In the chat, Enter sends and Shift+Enter is a new line. ⌘S saves Rules, Policy or an automation. Tab indents in the Rules editor (Save's tooltip says so, and that Esc leaves it, onto Save) and moves focus everywhere else: a row is a button, so Tab-focusing selects it and Enter or Space opens it. Menus, the reviewers box, settings fields and the hotkey recorder (Record's tooltip names its keys) take their arrows, Enter, Space, Backspace and Esc (→ into Snooze in a row's menu); in the filter box Esc clears it, and Enter or ↓ leaves it. The desktop app keeps its show or hide hotkey (Control+Alt+D) and ⌘, for Settings.

Nothing outside a box you're typing in writes to GitHub or changes an item.
