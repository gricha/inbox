# UI

The main page is what needs you: one ordered list in a few parts, one line per item. A PR's review opens in its place and Esc brings the list back. Later holds what was sorted away, Merging your own PRs Claude is getting merged, and Settings is configuration.

## Principles

- **Only what needs you on the main page.** If it's on the main page, you need to act on it: one list, what the desk put in now and then today, in the desk's order (`src/desk.ts` orders it; the page only renders it, so the tray, MCP and notifications say the same). No now-vs-today split. A quiet heading over each part, in order: agents' questions; Back to you: your PRs the coordinator handed back, and your ready PRs nobody's asked to review; other people's PRs waiting on your review (asked of you, or pushed to since your review; not drafts): you're blocking them; issues and other people's PRs that only mention you or were handed to you; your own PRs (drafts to mark ready, replies, fixes). Back to you and Waiting on your review have amber headings. The desk says each card's part (`group`) and sorts Now and Today each by part (so the tray and `desk_list` agree); the page shows one part at a time, its Now before its Today. Each heading counts its lines ("1 of 7" while filtering), and the "n more" line says what each part has behind it ("12 more: 2 waiting on your review, 3 issues and other PRs, 7 of your PRs"). Someone else's PR comes onto it once Claude has reviewed it; one quiet line under the list says how many are still being reviewed.
- **One line per item, as a table.** The rows sit on one sheet with a hairline between them. Each says whether it's a PR or an issue (a small grey label in its own column), the title (15px), then quietly its repo#number (a long repo name is cut short, the number never; and a stack's place, "3/20"), at most one call to action naming the action, then who it's from ("by you" a shade darker), a PR's stage and its age in columns of one width on the right. Below 760px of table the stage column goes, below 520px who it's from. No why, AI gist, concerns, verdict, chips or "sorted by Claude": Enter opens the review (or an issue in place), where all that is, and the keys are in the help overlay (`?`). The call to action is the desk's (`cta` on the card), a button before the age, tinted with the accent and filled with it under the cursor: your own open draft's "**a** Ready for review" (red CI too: the coordinator fixes CI once it's ready) (`a` marks it ready on GitHub at once, and it leaves your desk), "Reply" on your own PR whose only action is answering someone (Enter opens its review with the first reply box focused: ⌘↩ sends it, Esc leaves it), or "**a** Add reviewers" on your ready PR nobody's asked to review (`a` opens its review with the Reviewers box focused).
- **Agents' questions lead it.** A Claude session that needs you asks in the desk (`desk_inquire`), not in chat: its question is a row in the Questions part at the top, labeled **Agent** in the accent, with who asks after it and **Answer** as its call to action; `d` dismisses it from the row. Enter opens its page (`#/inquiry/<id>`), made to decide from without opening anything else: who asks and when, the question as its title, the PR or issue it's about (who it's from, its stage and whether it's resolved when the desk holds it, wherever it shows it; Open its review for a PR; GitHub on `o`; a review opened from here goes back to the question), the agent's context in paragraphs (its `code` spans and URLs kept, never markup), its links with where each goes, and the answers as one table, each with what the agent does next and its pick marked; `1`–`6` picks one, `w` writes your own (⌘↵ sends), `d` dismisses it. Answered or dismissed, it leaves the queue at once while the page stays and says so (✓ Answered, then who has it); a refusal brings it back with what you wrote. The desk refuses a question without enough context, without a link or the item it's about, or with an answer that doesn't say what follows, so every one arrives with that.
- **What you do takes it off.** Approve, a comment, Ready for review, Request changes, close, merge and Hand off for merge each take the item off its list at once (done until someone else acts) and into the Archive, or Merging for a hand-off, with a spinner in the toast until GitHub has it; if GitHub refuses, it comes back with the reason. A review stays open: in place of the key you pressed it says what you did and where it went ("✓ Approved · in Archive"), so you can read on, comment, or go on to the next with Esc. Nothing asks for a second press.
- **Merging is the coordinator's.** A PR you handed off (`m`) never shows on the main page. A draft isn't handed off: Ready for review is yours, so it would wait on you there; nor is a ready PR nobody's asked to review, since nobody would review it (one in Merging that becomes so comes back). The coordinator, a background Claude Code session the desk starts and wakes, gets it merged; only a PR it hands back comes back to the main page, on top.
- **Calm, informational.** No highlighter. Ink and pencil grey on the paper; colour only where it carries information: "CI failing" red, "changes requested" and "merge conflict" amber, the same words and colours in a review, and an AI review's risk (low green, medium amber, high red). Calls to action take the accent: the one to press in each place is filled (Approve or Ready for review in a review, Send, Request changes, Hand off for merge on your own PR, Merging's Review, a row's call to action under the cursor), a row's off the cursor is tinted. On a row and in Merging the rest (Ask, More, Merging's other keys) are text with their key, no box, so there the one to press is the only shape; a review's few actions (its call to action and More) are all buttons. What a Merging row waits on is all ink and pencil. Sentence case, no chips or boxed badges, no all-caps, no monospace outside code. The selected row is a neutral fill, nothing more.
- **Themes are per device.** Tokens live on `:root` in `web/src/styles.css`: the light set, the dark set under `prefers-color-scheme` (System), and `[data-theme]` sets for Light, Dark, Sand (warm paper) and Slate (blue-grey dark). Settings → Appearance picks one for this device (`web/src/theme.ts`, never a desk setting): the desktop app keeps it in its own data (the UI's origin changes when it runs from a checkout), and paints the window and its start page in that theme's paper; a browser keeps it in localStorage. `index.html` applies it before the first paint. Text colours are `--ink`, `--ink-2` and `--pencil`; `--faint` is for non-text only. `--accent` fills a call to action under `--on-accent`; `--accent-ink` on `--accent-wash` tints one. Text and button labels meet WCAG AA in every theme. Check every change in light and dark and each theme (`npm run shots`).

## Layout

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Review Desk  │ [ Filter / ]                                        Synced 1m  │
│              ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ GitHub    21 │ Waiting on your review 12                                      │  a part's heading, in amber
│ Later        │ Support HTTP/2 prior knowledge  tinyhttp#212                29h │
│ Merging    2 │ Retry dead-lettered jobs with jittered backoff  worker#882  26h │  the selected row: a neutral fill
│              │ …                                                              │
│              │ 12 more: 2 waiting on your review, 8 of your PRs               │
│ Settings   , │                                                                │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- A 232px sidebar: GitHub (the main page, with how many need you), Later (no count: nothing there needs you), Merging (no count either: nothing there needs you; the tooltip has how many are merging) and Settings. The active item is a neutral filled pill; a review is under the page it was opened from. At 1000px and below it hides; the top bar then names the page and carries quiet "Later" and "Merging 2" links.
- The main page, Later, Merging and a review use the window's width, with the page's side padding.
- While a page waits on the desk, it keeps its layout: quiet bars (`--wash`, no shimmer, a slow breath) where its rows or text will be, shown only once the wait passes about 150 ms, so a quick answer never flashes them. The main page, Later and Merging show rows at a row's height, Settings a section, an issue opened in place its lines. Empty, each page is one pencil line: "Nothing needs you right now.", "Nothing sorted away.", "Nothing in Merging yet: …".
- Every page ends near the window's bottom: below its last line there's only the room j/k leave under a row (84px), never empty paper to scroll into. `npm run shots` checks the queue, a review, Merging and Settings scrolled to the bottom.
- The main page: its first `queue.cards` rows (10) in part order, then one pencil line, "12 more: …", which j/k stop on and Enter (or a click) opens in place; it folds only when two or more would go behind it. Empty, it's one line: "Nothing needs you right now." While Claude can't log in, one line above the rows says so once: "**Claude is paused:** Claude Code isn't logged in, so nothing is being sorted or reviewed. Run `claude auth login`." (or "**AI reviews are paused:**" and why).
- A stack of PRs is one line, the one the desk sends for it (the lowest member in the most urgent place), with its place among the stack's open PRs quietly after its repo#number: "api#4154 3/6". A row under Later in a stack says it the same way.
- The desk's order: what the coordinator handed back; a close look or high risk on the current commit, then on an earlier one; what a cap made not urgent; your PR approved and green; the rest as the rules ordered them; someone else's PR with CI failing, which waits on its author; "Skip it" last. Now's before Today's, each in that order, within each part (above).
- The quiet rule: an item nobody has touched for `queue.quiet_days` (14) leaves the main page for Later, before caps. A pinned item and a review you were asked for in that time stay.
- Enter (or a click) on a PR opens its review (below). On an issue it opens the item in place under its row: why it's here ("Assigned to you"), one pencil line of facts (by, how you were asked, labels, comments, who placed it), where it sits and from which board, a collapsed rule trace, its recent activity (the last 8 events in plain words), and its keys under a hairline (esc Close, w Comment while it's open, o, e, s, m); `w` opens a comment bar at the window's bottom, where the coordinator's composer opens (⌘↵ sends it to GitHub and the issue leaves your desk until someone else acts; Esc closes it; a refusal keeps your words in it, and a refresh that moves the row doesn't touch it). `w` opens any row in place with the full table of signals, its rule trace and its raw signals. The snooze menu (`s`) opens under the row: 1–5 for a time or until activity, then from 6 what the PR can wait on now (`wake_on`: CI passes, new commits, it's approved, it's ready for review, the merge conflict is fixed). A right-click on any row (the queue, Later, the Archive, Merging) opens its menu at the pointer: Review, Open on GitHub, its call to action or Hand off, Done (Back on the desk when cleared), Snooze (to the side, the same options), Mute, Pin, Ask the coordinator, and the More menu's GitHub writes; each shows its key, which also works while the menu is open, with arrows, → into Snooze and Esc.
- Later (`#/later`, `g l`): the sorted-away groups, Later (open with the page), FYI, Hidden by rules and Snoozed or muted, each a heading with its count that opens and closes it (a folded one steps back to pencil; `g f`, `g h`, `g x` open one from anywhere). Their rows are the same one line plus, in pencil, why it's there: Later's why or "quiet for 3 weeks", a snoozed row's "snoozed until Sat 9:00" or "snoozed until CI passes".
- Archive (`#/archive`, `g a`, last in the sidebar): what's done, newest first: what you did that took it off your desk ("done: back when someone else acts", with when you did it as its time) and what the world dealt with (its reason, "merged", "you approved", "approved by alice", with when it resolved), the last day's that were on the main page or that you opened or resolved yourself. `d` on a row puts it back on your desk. While filtering, groups with matches open on their own and empty ones disappear. Esc goes back to the main page.
- Claude sorts every item; a hard rule is a floor. Who placed it is in the opened item's facts and the rule trace, never on the row.
- Settings: one scrolling page, wider than the queue so the rules editor fits, with no page title (the sidebar names it; one pencil line says where it's saved) and section heads at 17px. Each setting is one column, at every width: its label, its help at a reading measure (64ch), then its control, with space and no rule between settings. Sections come from the schema in `src/settings.ts`, plus Rules (the TOML editor with preview, and beside it the saved config's hard rules in plain lines: "PRs to review (Personal) → Now: someone asked you directly" over its `when` in pencil, the space named when two boards share a name; with none, one line says Claude can sort anything off your desk); `#/settings/<section>` scrolls to one. Settings stays mounted once opened, so an unsaved Rules draft survives a trip to the queue.
- Settings → Policy, right after Rules: one sentence (how you want Claude to sort your desk), a plain text editor in the UI font, "⌘S Save" (saves the editor you're in) and Revert as an action line, like Rules', then quiet lines: while policy.md is empty the editor holds the starter and one line says "This is the starter policy. Claude sorts by it until you change it." (editing and saving makes it yours; clearing it brings the starter back), the judge's status (a failure in ink, not red) ("23 items sorted by Claude. 7 of 300 this hour.", or why it's off, or its last failure and the next try), and the file's path (the starter is said once, in the line under the editor). If the file changed since you opened it, Save keeps your text and says so.
- Settings → Agents: whether Claude Code has the desk's MCP tools and skill, and Connect when it doesn't; Coordinator turns the coordinator off (it's stopped and nothing wakes it); Coordinator skips permission prompts (on by default) runs it in bypass permissions mode (its deny list still holds), and changing it restarts the coordinator in the new mode.
- Settings → Automations, after Policy: one table of them, each row its switch (on: it runs on its schedule), its name, its schedule and next run (or "an agent wrote or changed it: read it, then turn it on to run it", with its instruction quoted under it), **Run now** and **Edit**, and under it its last run in a line ("Ran 2h ago: put 2 on your desk"; in red when it couldn't add something or failed). Clicking its name opens its instruction (quoted) and its runs: when and how it ran, the agent's summary (text only), each item it listed with what came of it (Added, Already on your desk, You'd dealt with it, Nobody needs it now, Couldn't add) and its why, linked when it's a PR or issue, and `claude --resume <id>` for what it did. **New automation** or Edit opens the editor: name, when, the instruction, ⌘S saves, Esc cancels, Delete (no confirmation; it stops a run in progress). A new one's id is its name's, made free with -2, -3… so it never replaces one you have; a switch leaves an open editor as it is. With none yet, one line says to ask an agent or write one.
- Settings → Appearance, before Desktop app: one Theme select (System, Light, Dark, Sand, Slate) for this device; it isn't a desk setting, so it isn't in `settings.json` and another window or device keeps its own.
- Settings → AI review: when AI reviews are paused after failures in a row (an expired login), one line says so, its label in ink as above the queue ("**AI reviews are paused:** … Next try in 30m."), within the measure, once, instead of on every PR. Every agent is your own `claude` from your PATH: there's no agent, command or model to set.

## The review view

`#/review/<id>`, Linear-style: the PR is read and approved in the desk, in the queue's place, with the sidebar still there. The diff takes the page's width; a foldable sidebar on the right holds the stack, the files and recent activity.

```
  api#4154  3/6  by priya                                         Stack on main                N P
  Planner: hash join stage                                        ○ #4153 Planner: run the table…  ✗
  asked you directly · CI passing · changes requested             ┃○ #4154 Planner: hash join st… ±┃
  +172 −32 in 3 files                                             ○ #4155 Planner: stream partia…
                                                                  ○ #4156 Planner: spill to disk   ✗
  (threads waiting on you)                                          2 more
  [ Comment on api#4154… (w)                    ]
                                                                  3 files                      n p
  Look closely  ▮▮▯ medium risk                                   src/planner/plan.ts       • Look
                                                                  src/routes/query.ts       • Look
  Adds a streaming scan (streamRows) and uses it in runPlan, ...  docs/api/query.md           Skip
                                                                  Recent activity
  [a Approve]  [· More]                                           sam commented · 37m

  Look  src/planner/plan.ts  budget checked only between batches  +7 −3
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 17 + export async function runPlan(q, budgetMs = ...)        │
  │    ┃ The budget is only checked between batches, so ...      │  an AI note, at its line
```

- Opening it never waits: the review draws at once from its row's card (the header with CI, the review state and size, and the AI review's read when the row carries one) and quiet bars where the conversation, the keys, the diff and the sidebar's files will be, in their places, so nothing moves when the rest lands. Resting on a PR's row (j/k or the pointer, 150 ms) fetches its review ahead (`?ahead=1`, which starts no AI review or read), and the desk fetches the main page's diffs ahead after each sync, so opening one is usually instant. A big diff draws its first files with the page and the rest a chunk at a time.
- Header: the ref, a stack's place ("3/6", as on the main page), author ("by you" on yours), title, then one line: why it's on the desk (the row doesn't say; a fact it already says is left out), CI, the review state ("approved by marco"), a merge conflict (only while it's open), size. Your PR in Merging says "Merging", with what it waits on and the coordinator's latest note and its age under it, in pencil, as Merging does. Your own open draft offers "**a** Ready for review" first, as its row does, and no hand-off. The sidebar has its own "**f** Hide" at its top; folded, a "**f** Files" (or "Stack and files") button sits at the top right of the page. Its keys go in Merging's order, `b` last. Esc and the top bar's back link ("‹ Queue", "‹ Later" or "‹ Merging") go back to the page it was opened from. Problems are coloured words, and pencil at regular weight once the PR is sorted away, resolved, or calm in Merging. A wrapped line starts with a fact, never a "·", and a fact that wraps carries on after its "·", not under it.
- A resolved PR (Enter on a row under Resolved, or one you just approved) says so in one pencil line under the facts, "Resolved: merged by li-wei · 2h" (the reason, who merged or closed it, and how long ago, "just now" in the first minute), in place of the why.
- What reviewers said, under the header and before the AI review (with none, nothing): "Waiting on your reply" and how many, then each one as a margin note: who, where (file:line, "outdated", or "on the PR") and when in pencil, Claude's one-line gist in ink, its take with the call in semibold ("**Valid concern:** …", "**Only you can answer:** …"), the original words behind "What azulus wrote", and a reply box that starts with Claude's suggested reply. Send (or ⌘↩) posts it as you. Until Claude has read one, the gist is the comment's first line and a pencil line says "Claude is reading it."; a failure says why in ink. Everything not waiting on you is one quiet line, "3 more conversations not waiting on you", that opens to a line each (who started it, where, resolved, the last comment's start), each opening to its comments.
- The AI review, with no heading: its verdict leads it, a step under the title (only "Look closely" is set large, in ink; "Skim it" in ink-2 and "Skip it" in pencil are a size down), then its risk in colour at nearly the verdict's size, with a three-step meter (low green, medium amber, high red), then "on an earlier commit" in pencil when it is; the model and time are in the verdict's tooltip. Then the summary. Each concern is also a note in the code, so the concerns are listed (as margin notes) only when no notes can sit there: none, or a review of an earlier commit. While it's being written, a quiet line says so ("Claude is reviewing it. The notes land in the code below in a minute or two."); a failure shows its reason in ink, not red (it's about the agent, not the PR), with a command in it set as code (`claude auth login`), and `i` tries again. When AI reviews are paused after failures in a row, a PR that wasn't tried says that. An open PR with none says "No AI review yet. Press i to ask Claude for one."; a merged or closed PR with none says "No AI review." (`i` still asks, though the key line leaves it out); your own PR, open or not, shows the section only once a review exists or is being written. A re-review adds "**Since your review:** …" under the summary. A review of an earlier commit stays, marked "on an earlier commit" beside its risk, and its notes aren't placed in the new code.
- The diff in the desk's reading order (`ReviewView.order`), the page's width: what Claude ranked first, a look before a skim, each a hunk under its file's name with its level ("Look" in ink, "Skim" a step quieter) and Claude's few words why; then what Claude didn't read ("Not read", and whether the PR or its file was too long); then what Claude left out, each "Skip" with no why, in view: its answer comes from a diff anyone can write, so it can't hide code; then one pencil line, "Not worth reviewing · 2 files", over what the desk itself folds (lockfiles and generated files, files with no text diff), which `n` stops on and Enter (or a click) opens in place. A file's hunks next to each other at one level read as one. A review without a ranking (none yet, older, or for an earlier commit) keeps file order, and a file with no text diff says so in its header. `n`/`p` step through that order, `]`/`[` through the notes in it. A PR with no changed files says "No files changed."
- The sidebar (`f` folds and opens it, for this device, kept in localStorage; 280px beside the diff above 1100px, over the diff's right edge below it, where it starts folded):
  - The stack, when the PR is in one: "Stack on main", then its open PRs bottom first (the line through this PR; other branches at a fork under "Other branches"; merged and closed ones are left out, so "3/6" is the third row), each ○, its number and title, and only what's news: CI failing ✗ in red, changes requested ± in amber, approved ✓. The PR on screen wears the cursor's fill and ring; the words, a draft's included, are in each one's tooltip. A long stack shows the PR below this one, this one and the next two, then one quiet line, "16 more", that opens the rest, so the files stay in view. A click, or `N` (next, the row under it) and `P` (previous), opens that one's review.
  - The files, each once in reading order with its level (Look, Skim, Not read, Skip) and a dot when it has notes; a click goes to where reading reaches it. A long folder gives way from its start ("…versions/0008_cache.py"), as in a file's header, so each file's name stays whole right after it. The file you're reading follows the scroll and wears the cursor's fill and ring.
  - Recent activity: the PR's latest events in the queue's words (never the desk's own "first seen"), newest first, three of them and the rest behind a quiet "n more", each with its time after a "·". A resolved PR leaves out its "resolved" event and the one that caused it (the merge, the approval, the removed request); the Resolved line says it.
- Diffs: `@pierre/diffs`, unified, word-level changes, wrapped lines, Shiki highlighting in Pierre's light and dark themes (the one the device's theme is: System follows the computer, Sand is light, Slate dark), on `--sheet` in a hairline frame. Changed lines are tinted with the theme's own `--green` and `--red`, and unchanged lines fold into a full-width band on `--paper` ("14 unmodified lines"). Its CSS variables take the app's fonts (JetBrains Mono for code). Each file's name sticks under the top bar while you read it.
- AI notes are margin notes under the line they explain: a thin rule, the note in the UI font. The one `]`/`[` landed on gets the cursor's fill and ring. Notes about a whole file sit above its diff.
- Review threads sit under the line they're on, after any AI note there, as the same margin notes: each comment's author and age, then its text (GitHub text, shown as text), then Reply and On GitHub. A resolved one is one pencil line ("Resolved · priya: …") that opens. Outdated threads are only in the conversation above.
- Commenting on a line: hovering a line shows a `+` by its number; a click opens a box under the line, after anything already on it (a drag down the numbers takes the range, within one hunk, marked in a quiet grey while you drag), and ⌘↩ or Comment posts it on GitHub at once, as you: one comment, as GitHub's "Add single comment", on the commit whose diff you picked the line in, so a push while you write doesn't move it. Esc in an empty box closes it; with words in it, Esc leaves the box and keeps them, and another `+` (or Reply), or a key that acts (`a`, `d`, `m`, `b`, `w`, `.`), takes you back to them instead, opening their file if it's folded, so nothing you type can slip out of the box and approve; an empty box moves to the line you pick. Leaving the review keeps unsent words, in this window, for when you're back on it. While a box you're typing in is drawn again (a refresh regrouped its file), keys are swallowed rather than run as shortcuts. While one sends, the box is read-only and `+` on that PR waits, even in the review opened again; if GitHub refuses it, it says so (even after you've left) and the words come back to that PR's review. A comment pins to the commit the diff on screen is at, which can be newer than the desk's last sync. A box whose line or thread a push took off the diff moves above the diff, still yours to send or cancel. Reply on a thread opens the same box right under it (a resolved one stays open). What you sent shows in place, with a link, until the refreshed conversation has it. It's part of reviewing, not its end: the PR stays on your desk and Approve and Request changes stay.
- Folding files: a file's header (its chevron, level and name) folds its diff to the header, and every part of that file with it; `z` folds the file you're in, `Z` folds them all or opens them all; either way the file you were in stays in view and stays the one `z` means. Folded files in a row read as one list. `]`/`[` to a note in a folded file opens it. A different PR starts with every file open.
- Approve: a green button, as Hand off for merge is on yours; `a` sends it at once, and the PR leaves your desk (above). The key shows only on someone else's open PR; it's disabled, with the reason as its tooltip, on one you already approved at this commit. Under what reviewers said, an open PR has a box for your comment on it (`w` puts the cursor there); ⌘↩ sends it, and the PR leaves your desk until someone answers (a refusal keeps your words in the box; with words in it, a key that acts, `a d m b .`, goes back to the box instead). More (`.`) has Open on GitHub (`o`) and the GitHub writes the PR allows. The sidebar has its own Hide (`f`); hidden, a small Files (or Stack and files) button sits where it was. It approves the commit on screen: if the desk has seen a newer push, it refuses. The approval is one of the desk's writes to GitHub, with a reply you send and Ready for review ([api.md](api.md#security-model)).
- Your draft's reviewers: a line above Ready for review. Those already asked show first and stay; you add more in a box (`@` focuses it) that offers GitHub's suggested reviewers, then people with access and the org's teams as you type (↑/↓, Enter adds, Backspace on an empty box takes the last off, Esc leaves it). After the box, "Suggested" and GitHub's suggested reviewers you haven't picked, up to four, each a dashed pill: a click adds it. Ready for review asks them right after it marks the PR ready. On your ready PR nobody's asked to review, the same line shows with the box focused when it opens, and the one filled button is "**a** Ask reviewers", which asks who you picked (with nobody picked, `a` puts the cursor in the box).

## Merging

`#/inflight`: your own open PRs you handed off with `m`, which the coordinator is getting merged, one line each like the main page's, at the window's width. Open ones first, the latest activity on top; merged and closed ones sit under Merged for 3 days, pencil rows like Resolved's.

- Each row: the title (as the main page's), its ref, a stack's place ("2/6"), one phrase, its age. The phrase is what it waits on, the desk's words ("waits on a CI fix", "waits on a second look from dana", "ready to merge"), then the coordinator's latest note ("Pushed 4be71c2 for dana's 3 comments", at 1000px and below only on the selected row); it stays on one line and gives way at its end. Every title takes the same share of the row and every ref one width, so refs and phrases start in one column, selected or not. All ink and pencil, problems included: the coordinator has it. No highlighter.
- Above the rows, one quiet line about the coordinator: "Coordinator", **Watch on claude.ai** (its Remote Control page, in the browser), `c` copy attach command (the command is its tooltip), `w` wake it; when it can't run, "**The coordinator can't run:**" in red and the reason in ink-2; before it's started, "The coordinator starts on your first hand-off; w starts it now."; off, that it's off in Settings → Agents.
- A stack's rows sit together, bottom first, under one quiet line ("Stack on main · 6 open") when two or more of its PRs are here.
- The selected row keeps only its title, ref and age; its phrase moves whole to the line under it, with the note's age, then its keys, all on the row's fill: Review (filled with the accent), then Open on GitHub, Back to desk and Ask coordinator as text.
- Nothing here reaches the main page. Only a PR the coordinator hands back goes there, on top, and leaves Merging.
- Empty, the page is one line: "Nothing in Merging yet: press m on one of your PRs when you're done with it."

## Keys

`j/k` move (they stop on "n more") · `Enter` review a PR, open an issue, show the rest · `o` GitHub · `a` the row's call to action (Ready for review on your draft, Add reviewers on your PR nobody's asked to review) · `m` hand your PR off for merge · `e`/`d` done (restore on a cleared item) · `s` snooze, then 1–5 (or 6 on: until it happens) · `m` mute · `p` pin · `u` undo (only what this window did) · `i` AI review · `w` rule trace and signals (on an open issue opened in place: comment) · `/` filter · `Esc` close, then clear the filter (on Later, back to the main page) · `r` sync · `,` settings · `g r` rules · `g m` Merging · `g l/f/d/h/x` Later with that group open (the key after `g` is always the chord's, never the key's own action) · `?` help, which lists them all.

In a review: `n/p` next and previous change, in reading order · `N/P` next and previous PR in the stack · `f` the sidebar · `]/[` next and previous AI note · `z`/`Z` fold the file you're in, or all · `+` by a line's number, comment on it · `a` approve (or, on your own draft, Ready for review; on your PR nobody's asked to review, Ask reviewers) · `@` add a reviewer to your draft, or to your PR nobody's asked to review · `w` comment on the PR · `m` hand off, `b` back to desk · `.` More · `o` GitHub · `i` AI review again · `Esc` back to where you opened it.

In Merging: `j/k` move · `Enter` review · `o` GitHub · `b` back to desk · `c` copy `claude attach <id>` · `w` wake the coordinator · `Esc` the main page. `e`, `s` and `m` don't apply there: the coordinator has it, so `b` brings it back first.

On every page: `t` opens the composer to the coordinator (below).

Tab focus and the j/k cursor are the same thing: focusing a row selects it.

## Asking the coordinator

`t` anywhere, or a quiet **t Ask** on the selected row's line (before its call to action), **Ask coordinator** under each conversation waiting on you in a review (the action line is full), and **t Ask coordinator** under a selected row in Merging, opens a small bar at the window's bottom, over the page. One pencil line says what it's about: the open review's PR and the conversation you were last on in it ("dana's thread on src/auth.ts:42"), the selected row, or nothing. Enter (or **↵ Send**) sends it, Esc (or **esc Close**) closes it, from wherever focus is. Your latest three asks about the same item (or about nothing) show above it, each with the coordinator's answer under it once it lands; until then a line says what it's doing, looked at every 10 s: "On it" with a spinner, "Queued" behind what it's on, "Waiting on a permission prompt" (answer it on claude.ai), or "It stopped without answering" with **Wake it**, and a link to watch it on claude.ai. A coordinator that won't start says so there, and your words stay in the box. With the coordinator off, the bar says so and sends nothing. A PR's review lists your asks about it and their answers under "You asked the coordinator", the latest three and "n earlier" for the rest.
