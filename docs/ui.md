# UI

One surface. The window is the queue; a PR's review opens in its place and Esc brings the queue back. In flight lists your own PRs that agents are keeping moving, and Settings is configuration.

## Principles

- **Minimize surfaces, maximize automation.** One queue; boards and rules feed it and are edited in Settings → Rules. The sidebar navigates the queue, In flight and Settings; it never categorizes by repo or board.
- **Only urgency gets colour.** A yellow highlighter marks what's urgent (the now bucket): its ref, never a heading. It's a marker stroke (`::before`, chisel-cut caps sized in em, a wavy middle, a slight tilt), never a box; `npm run shots` checks it covers its text and is never clipped. Everything else is ink and pencil grey. Problems are coloured words on every card in the queue, the same words and colours everywhere (the queue, a review, the lane): "CI failing" red, "changes requested" and "merge conflict" amber; they turn grey once sorted away. Passing CI, a verdict, an agent's failure: ink and pencil; "high risk" is the AI's one red word.
- **No boxes around words.** No chips, tags or outlined badges. Cards are sheets on the paper, never outlined. The only frame is the keyboard cursor: a neutral fill with a pencil ring, on a card as on a row (and on a key you Tab to); any other focus ring is pencil, never blue. A link in a sentence is ink-2 over a quiet rule, and a switch that's on is ink: blue is only the new dot. A key is a pencil letter before what it does ("**esc** Close", "**1** 1 hour"), never boxed and never after its label.
- **Sentence case.** No all-caps labels, no monospace outside code.
- **Both themes.** Tokens live on `:root` in `web/src/styles.css`, with a dark block under `prefers-color-scheme`. Check every change in light and dark. Text colours are `--ink`, `--ink-2` and `--pencil`; `--faint` is for non-text only.

- A 232px sidebar: GitHub (the queue, with its count; the tooltip says how many are urgent), In flight (with how many PRs are in flight, as its heading says; the ones that need you are in the queue) and Settings, counts in pencil. The active item is a neutral filled pill; a review is under the page it was opened from. At 820px and below it hides; the top bar then names the page and carries a quiet "In flight 3" link.
- One centred column beside it: 800px for the queue and In flight, so the AI's call on a card's right edge sits near its title. The top bar's filter starts on the column's edge. In the queue, In flight and the coordinator's log, headings and every card's text start on one edge.
- Every page ends near the window's bottom: below its last line there's only the room j/k leave under a card (84px), never empty paper to scroll into, a review's last file included (`n` there scrolls as far as the page goes). `npm run shots` checks the queue, a review, In flight and Settings scrolled to the bottom.
- The queue is prefiltered, not a list of GitHub: one list of what the rules and Claude put in now and today, now's first, every card carries its pre-flight, and everything else is one quiet line below.
- A card in the queue: the title, whole and wrapping, with the new dot and pin after its last word and its age on the right. Then the ref, why it's here and its problems in their colours (left out when the why says them, in any words: "conflicts with main" says "merge conflict", "red CI" says "CI failing", "you requested changes" says "changes requested"), and on the right edge the size in pencil (not on your own PR) and the AI's call: "Look closely" (semibold ink), "Skim it" (ink-2), "Skip it" (pencil), the review's own words, with "high risk" in red before it. Refs read down the left and calls down the right. Then the AI's gist, as long as the call is heavy: whole sentences at a 72ch measure, up to about 200 characters and three lines to look closely, 140 and two lines to skim, the first clause on one line to skip (the card's full width; with no clause in reach, it ends at the last "and", "with" or word that fits, with a period, never an ellipsis). On a PR pushed since your review, the gist is what changed since you looked, "**Since your review:** moves /v1/export onto token auth too…" (the review has the whole summary). A card the AI says to look at closely, or calls high risk, adds its top concern as a margin note (a thin rule, no box): its first sentence, consequence included, never clipped. A number keeps its unit on its line ("15 s", "0–100 %"). An issue's third line is its latest event, in pencil ("tomas commented · 4h"); then its why says how you're on it ("assigned to you", "you were mentioned") instead of only that something is new, and it carries no new dot. A comment's text isn't shown: it's GitHub text the event log, which agents read, doesn't keep.
- Your own PR's why is what you owe: a real label in ink, the rest regular ("**Babysitter asks:** keep it nightly, or switch to hourly?", "**In flight:** reply ready to send", "**Approved and green:** merge it"), and a why without a label in regular ink. Its third line is only what a card's keys don't already say: the reply waiting for Send, as a margin note that starts with who it answers in pencil ("To dana:", then its first sentence; selected, "To dana on src/export/audit.ts:88: "Should the cursor be opaque?"" on its own line and the whole reply, so `y` twice never sends what the card hasn't shown), or who last spoke on it and the key that deals with it ("priya commented · 2h · **d** hands it to a babysitter"), or what failed ("Acceptance failing"), less what its why already says; selected, the keys say what d does, so that part goes. In flight, what failed goes with the problem word, as in the lane ("CI failing: node 22, lint"), and a PR with no clone to work in says so as its why ("**No babysitter:** CI failing: node 22, lint") with one link under it, "Set up a local clone of oss/tinyhttp" (Settings → In flight, which Enter opens too). A babysitter's question leaves the PR's problems to the lane. A failing check its base branch doesn't require isn't "CI failing": it's a pencil "optional check failing".
- No current review: the call's place says "AI review being written", "AI review queued" or "AI review paused", in pencil. A review of an earlier commit keeps its call with "· earlier commit" (whether a new one is being written, waits or is off), and its gist and concern turn pencil. While Claude can't log in (the AI review, the judge or the coordinator failed on it), one line above the queue says so once: "**Claude is paused:** Claude Code isn't logged in, so nothing is being sorted or reviewed. Run `claude auth login`." When only AI reviews keep failing for another reason, it says "**AI reviews are paused:**" and why.
- The selected card has a neutral fill and the pencil ring, and opens in place: about four lines of the summary (Enter opens the review with the rest), up to two concerns, one pencil line of facts ("by priya", unless its stack's head says it; "CODEOWNERS ping to @ml-team" when nobody asked you by name; CI passing or running, unless the why says green; approved, threads, draft, risk below high, "5 notes on the changes", who placed it ("placed by a hard rule", "sorted by Claude", "not sorted by Claude yet", "Claude gave no answer"; nothing for your PR in flight), a demotion like "3 from PRs to review are urgent already"; less what the card already says), then its keys under a hairline ("enter Review o Open on GitHub e Done s Snooze m Mute"; your PR adds "d Hand to a babysitter", "d Put in flight" with babysitting off; approved and green, "o Merge on GitHub" comes first; one in flight has only Send (`y`, with a reply), Answer in a terminal (`c`) and Dismiss the question (`x`) with a question, Show in flight, Open on GitHub and Take back, in that order, as in the lane and its review). `y` arms Send: the key reads "**y** again to send" in ink semibold and a toast says "Press y again to send", a second `y` within 3 s posts it, anything else lets it lapse. j/k move focus with the cursor, so Tab goes on to the selected card's keys, from the first card the queue selects on load too; a key under the keyboard wears the cursor's fill and ring. A group's first card brings its heading into view. j/k keep the card they land on where it was while the one they left closes above it; near the top, where the page can't scroll up that far, it moves up by the rest, so the cards never leave a gap between them. A card that grows past the window's bottom then scrolls into view. A click opens a card without selecting it first, since the selected card grows. The snooze menu opens under the card, never over its keys.
- Stacks: three or more consecutive items with the same author and reason sit under one line ("3 PRs from vaind asked you directly", the problem in its colour) on one sheet, ruled apart; their cards leave the why out. Your own are "Your 7 PRs" wherever they are, sorted away too. A stack of your own PRs is "Your 3 PRs **CI failing** **d** hands each to a babysitter", and each PR in it is two lines: its title, and its ref with what failed.
- The order: now's cards, then today's, each the weightiest first: your PR with a reply to send or a question; a close look or high risk (one on the current commit before one on an earlier commit); an item a cap made not urgent; your PR approved and green; then the rest as the rules ordered them, your PR in flight that no key finishes (no clone) among them; someone else's PR with CI failing, which waits on its author; "Skip it" last. Over a cap, the lightest call gives up its slot first: skip ("the AI says skip it"), then skim, then not reviewed yet, so a close look stays urgent; the rest say "3 from PRs to review are urgent already" (over the today cap, "the queue has 6 from PRs to review already", in Later).
- The fold: the queue shows its first `queue.cards` cards (10); the rest sit behind one pencil line, "6 more", which also says what behind it shouldn't wait ("13 more, 1 urgent, 1 pinned"), with the next cards' edges showing under the last one (a step lighter than a sheet in dark). It folds only when two or more would go behind it: one card costs less than the line. A stack is all above the line or all behind it, and counts as one card. j/k stop on it (the cards' cursor, the column's full width) and Enter (or a click) opens the rest in place.
- An empty queue is one line: "**Queue** Nothing needs you right now." with no 0.
- The quiet rule: an item nobody has touched for `queue.quiet_days` (14) leaves the queue for Later, before caps, with "quiet for 3 weeks" as its note. A pinned item and a review you were asked for in that time stay.
- Sorted away: later and resolved, on one quiet line with no counts. FYI, hidden by rules and cleared aren't named there: they open on their chords (`g f`, `g h`, `g x`) or the filter, and join the line while open. Opened, their items are flat rows on the paper, not cards, with regular-weight titles: title, then ref, why, problems in grey, each said once (and a note like "quiet for 3 weeks"). Each name opens its group inline (`g l`, `g d`); they're closed again on every load. While filtering, empty groups disappear, groups with matches open on their own, and nothing folds.
- Resolved lists the last day's items that were in the queue, or that you opened or resolved yourself; FYI, hidden and the rest of Later resolve silently. Its rows are dimmed like cleared ones (pencil titles), carry no "new" dot, and show the reason ("merged", "you approved", "approved by alice", "no longer requested") where the why goes, and when it resolved where the wait time goes, newest first. Acting on one (done, snooze) keeps it under Resolved.
- Enter (or a click) on a PR opens its review (below); on your PR in flight, which is only in the queue because it needs you, it opens In flight at that PR. On an issue it opens the item in place, in the selected card's shape: one pencil line of facts (by, how you were asked unless the why says it, labels, comments, last activity), one line of why only when it says more than the card's (for a resolved item, the reason first and then where it would be otherwise; the policy; a cap), a collapsed rule trace, its recent activity (the last 8 events in plain words: "alice approved · 2h", "CI went red · 20m", your own as "you"; never the desk's own "first seen", nor the event its why already says), and its keys under the hairline (esc Close, o, e, s, m). `w` opens any card in place with the full table of signals, its rule trace and its raw signals.
- Claude sorts every item: the card's why is Claude's (or the rule's, when Claude kept it or hasn't answered yet), its detail says "sorted by Claude", and the rule trace ends with a `Claude` step (`today, the tokenizer you maintain`, or `not sorted yet, so where the rules put it`). A hard rule is a floor, and its trace says so before Claude's step ("a hard rule: never below today"); where Claude went below it, the card keeps the rule's why and says "placed by a hard rule".
- The coordinator: when it changed the queue in the last day, the top bar carries one quiet pencil line, "Coordinator snoozed 2 items · 4m" ("just now" in the first minute; below 1000px the name goes and the verb stays, "Snoozed 2 items · 4m"). What it cleared says so under Cleared ("coordinator snoozed until Sat 9:00 or new activity"), and its reason is in the selected row's facts. Housekeeping that never reached you (Later, FYI) stays in its log and Settings → Agents. A click (`esc Close`) opens its log above the queue, on the cards' edge: one pencil line per run ("Run 14 · 6 events, 3 actions") above its actions, each with its reason and a pencil Undo, and its notes (not one that only repeats its run's single action), with the time on the right like a card's age; what it did outside a run (a policy edit in a terminal) sits under its own "In a terminal" line. A learn run reads "Run 13 · read your last 6 actions"; a policy change is "Changed the policy" with what it took out and put in under it, one pencil line each ("− …", "+ …", three at most), and a pencil Undo that puts the text before it back. No colour, no badge.
- Settings: one scrolling page, wider than the queue so the rules editor fits, with no page title (the sidebar names it; one pencil line says where it's saved) and section heads at the groups' 17px. Sections come from the schema in `src/settings.ts`, plus Rules (the TOML editor with preview, and beside it the saved config's hard rules in plain lines: "PRs to review (Personal) → Now: someone asked you directly" over its `when` in pencil, the space named when two boards share a name; with none, one line says Claude can sort anything off your desk); `#/settings/<section>` scrolls to one. Settings stays mounted once opened, so an unsaved Rules draft survives a trip to the queue.
- Settings → Policy, right after Rules: one sentence (how you want Claude to sort your desk; the coordinator refines it), a plain text editor in the UI font, "⌘S Save" (saves the editor you're in) and Revert as an action line, like Rules', then quiet lines: while policy.md is empty the editor holds the starter and one line says "This is the starter policy. Claude sorts by it until you change it." (editing and saving makes it yours; clearing it brings the starter back), the judge's status (a failure in ink, not red) ("23 items sorted by Claude. 7 of 300 this hour.", or why it's off, or its last failure and the next try), and the file's path (the starter is said once, in the line under the editor). If the file changed since you opened it, Save keeps your text and says so.
- Settings → Agents → Coordinator: the switch, one line of status ("Idle. Last run 5m ago: 6 events, 3 actions.", "Running, started 20s ago.", its last error and next try in ink, or why it's off), then its short session id and the command that opens it in a terminal, all of it, wrapping between arguments (`cd ~/.review-desk/coordinator && claude --resume <id> --mcp-config mcp.json`: your own Claude Code plus the desk's tools), with Copy.
- Settings → AI review and → In flight: when AI reviews or babysitters are paused after failures in a row (an expired login), one line says so, its label in ink as above the queue ("**AI reviews are paused:** … Next try in 30m."), within the measure, once, instead of on every PR. Every agent is your own `claude` from your PATH: there's no agent, command or model to set, and Settings → Agents only says whether Claude Code has the desk's tools and skill.

`#/review/<id>`, Linear-style: the PR is read and approved in the desk, in the queue's place, with the sidebar still there. The column widens to 1280px.

- Header: the ref (highlighted when it's urgent), author ("by you" on yours), title, then one line: why it's on the desk (a fact it already says is left out, as on the card), CI, the review state ("approved by marco"), a merge conflict (only while it's open), size. Your PR in flight says so in its why, worded as in the queue ("**In flight:** reply ready to send", or just "in flight" while its babysitter has it, as calm as its lane row), adds the babysitter's line from the lane under it, in pencil, unless it only repeats the why (a question, no clone: then the "Set up a local clone" link), and the reply waiting for Send as a margin note, which `y` (twice) sends and `x` discards from here too. With a question, `c` answers it in a terminal and `x` dismisses it, as in the queue and the lane; Copy prompt and AI review aren't offered on a PR in flight. Its keys go in the lane's order, `d` last. Esc and the top bar's back link ("‹ In flight" or "‹ Queue") go back to the page it was opened from. Problems are coloured words, as in the queue, and pencil at regular weight once the PR is sorted away or resolved. A wrapped line starts with a fact, never a "·", and a fact that wraps carries on after its "·", not under it.
- A resolved PR (Enter on a row under Resolved, or one you just approved) says so in one pencil line under the facts, "Resolved: merged by li-wei · 2h" (the reason, who merged or closed it, and how long ago, "just now" in the first minute), in place of the why; its ref loses the highlighter.
- Recent activity: the PR's latest events in the queue's words (never the desk's own "first seen"), newest first, three of them and the rest behind a quiet "n more", each with its time after the facts' "·". Above 1100px it's a column of its own beside the facts and the AI review, one event a line; narrower, it's one wrapped pencil line under the facts ("sam commented 37m · you were mentioned 1h · 2 more"), so the AI review and the diff stay near the top. A resolved PR leaves out its "resolved" event and the one that caused it (the merge, the approval, the removed request); the Resolved line says it.
- The AI review: who wrote it, by the model's name ("from Opus 5"), verdict, risk (a pencil fact; only high is red and semibold), summary, concerns as margin notes (as on the card and in the diff), and how many notes sit in the code. While it's being written, a quiet line says so; a failure shows its reason in ink, not red (it's about the agent, not the PR), with a command in it set as code (`claude auth login`), and `i` tries again. When AI reviews are paused after failures in a row, a PR that wasn't tried says that. A merged or closed PR with none says "No AI review." (`i` still asks, though the key line leaves it out); your own PR, open or not, shows the section only once a review exists or is being written. A re-review adds "**Since your review:** …" under the summary. A review of an earlier commit stays, marked "on an earlier commit" as on the card, and its notes aren't placed in the new code.
- Files on the left (sticky, 300px from 1280px up; above the diff at 1100px and below), with a dot on files that have notes. A long folder gives way from its start ("…versions/0008_cache.py") but always shows "…/", so each file's name stays whole right after it, wrapping when it's longer than the list. The file you're reading follows the scroll and wears the cursor's fill and pencil ring; at the page's end, that's the last file.
- Diffs: `@pierre/diffs`, unified, word-level changes, wrapped lines, Shiki highlighting in Pierre's light and dark themes, on `--sheet` in a hairline frame. Its CSS variables take the app's fonts (JetBrains Mono for code). Each file's name sticks under the top bar while you read it.
- AI notes are margin notes under the line they explain: a thin rule, the note in the UI font. The one `]`/`[` landed on gets the cursor's fill and ring. Notes about a whole file sit above its diff.
- Approve: `a` arms it (the key reads "**a** again to approve" in ink semibold, and a toast says "Press a again to approve"), a second `a` within 3 s sends it; anything else lets it lapse. The key shows only on someone else's open PR; it's disabled, with the reason as its tooltip, on one you already approved at this commit. It approves the commit on screen: if the desk has seen a newer push, it refuses. The approval is one of the desk's writes to GitHub, with Send on a drafted reply ([api.md](api.md#security-model)).

## In flight

`#/inflight`: your own open PRs you marked done with `d`, and what each babysitter is doing. One list, those that need you first, then the ones a babysitter is working on, then the quiet ones; merged and closed ones sit under Landed for 3 days, flat pencil rows like Resolved's.

```
  In flight 5
  Your PRs marked done. Babysitters fix CI and draft replies; a PR is back in the queue only when it needs you.
╭──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│ Paginate the audit log export                                                 3d │
│ api#4122  Reply ready to send   CI passing   changes requested by dana   …      │
│ pushed a fix for 3 comments · 4be71c2 · 24m                                      │
│ ┃ Reply to dana on src/export/audit.ts:88: "Should the cursor be opaque? …"      │
│ ┃ Kept it as the row id: the API docs promise a stable, sortable cursor, …       │
│ ┃ y Send   x Discard                                                             │
╰──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯
╭──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╮
│ Fix the flaky timezone test                                                  20h │
│ web#2295  CI failing: test (20.x)   waiting on marco                             │
│ fixing CI · 4m · npm test -- timezone                                            │
╰──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────╯
```

- Each row is a queue card, so a PR keeps its shape between the queue and the lane: the title and its age, then the ref, why it's back with you (as in the queue: a real label in ink, "**Babysitter asks:** …", "**Approved and green:** merge it"; otherwise regular ink, "Reply ready to send", "Comments from outside contributors", "No local clone of oss/tinyhttp, so no babysitter" with a link to set up local clones under it) and the PR's state; then, where a gist goes, the babysitter's own line ("fixing CI · 4m · npm test", "pushed a fix for 2 comments · abc1234", "watching") unless it only repeats the why. The PR's state in plain words: CI passing, failing (with up to two check names) or running; approved by, changes requested by, waiting on (people first, teams as a count: "waiting on azulus and 5 teams"); unresolved threads; merge conflict. A fact wraps only between its items. With babysitting off, the intro says so once, a PR that needs you has the problem itself as its why ("CI failing"), and nothing says "watching". The right-hand time is the queue's: how long it's been waiting, or when it landed.
- Calm: a row is ink and pencil grey while a babysitter has it, problems included. A row that needs you gets the highlighter on its ref and its problems their colour, as in the queue.
- A drafted reply sits under its row like an AI note in a review, a margin rule and no card: what it answers ("Your agent's reply to …" when an agent other than its babysitter drafted it) and the reply. Send (`y`, twice) and Discard (`x`) are the selected row's keys. Send posts it on GitHub as you; it's one of the desk's three writes, and `y` on its card in the queue or in its review sends the same reply.
- The selected row has the cards' one fill and ring, and its keys under the hairline, what finishes it first and Take back last, as in the queue and the review: Send and Discard (with a reply), or Answer in a terminal and Dismiss the question (with a question; Mark read for outside contributors' comments), then Review, Open on GitHub, Copy resume command (`cd <worktree> && claude --resume <session>`, to open the babysitter's session yourself) and Take back.
- In the queue, a PR in flight says so, its label in ink: "**In flight:** reply ready to send", or "**Babysitter asks:** …", so it doesn't read like a reviewer's ask; Enter on it opens the lane at that PR, `y` (twice) sends its reply from the queue, and with a question `c` copies the command to answer it in the babysitter's session and `x` dismisses it.
- Empty, the page is one line: "**In flight** Nothing yet: press d on one of your PRs when you're done with it."

## Keys

`j/k` move (they stop on "n more" lines) · `Enter` review a PR, open an issue, show the rest of the queue · `o` GitHub · `d` your PR in flight, or take it back · `y` then `y` send your in-flight PR's drafted reply · `x` dismiss its question (`c` copies the command to answer it) · `e` done (restore on a cleared item) · `s` snooze, then 1–5 · `m` mute · `p` pin · `u` undo (only what this window did) · `c` copy prompt · `i` AI review · `w` rule trace and signals · `/` filter · `Esc` close, then clear the filter · `r` sync · `,` settings · `g r` rules · `g i` in flight · `g l/f/d/h/x` sorted-away groups (the key after `g` is always the chord's, never the key's own action) · `?` help.

In a review: `n/p` next and previous file · `]/[` next and previous AI note · `a` then `a` approve · `d` your PR in flight, or take it back · `y` then `y` send your in-flight PR's reply, `x` discard it · `i` AI review again · `o` GitHub · `c` copy prompt · `Esc` back to where you opened it (the queue or In flight).

In flight: `j/k` move · `Enter` review · `o` GitHub · `d` take back · `y` then `y` send the drafted reply · `x` dismiss the question (or outside comments), or discard the reply · `c` copy the resume command · `Esc` the queue. `e`, `s` and `m` don't apply to a PR in flight: its babysitter has it, so `d` takes it back first.
