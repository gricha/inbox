## 1. Backtest (replay)

### Method

- **Window:** 2026-05-01 to 10-26, org getsentry. Read-only: ~880 GraphQL points, 0 REST search calls, ~60 REST GETs.
- **What you engaged with:** `is:pr org:getsentry {reviewed-by|commenter}:gricha -author:gricha updated:>=2026-05-01` returned 374 PRs. 359 had your first review or comment inside the window.
- **Population:** every PR created 2026-04-15 to 10-26 in seer, sentry, ops, getsentry and sandbox-router was scanned, not sampled. Searches were sliced by date to stay under 1,000 results each. 4,048 PRs not written by you had a machine-learning-ai ReviewRequestedEvent in the window.
- **Route:** half-monthly CODEOWNERS snapshots at historical commits, matched last-match-wins.
- **Affinity:** files from your merged getsentry PRs in the 120 days before each request (no future leak). Lock, generated and hub files are excluded; tests count half.
- **Replay:** 30-minute snapshots of the inbox, with caps. A card is the same author and commit scope within 72h. Signals are computed as of each snapshot. Tuned on May–Jul, tested on Aug–Oct.
- **Recall** = the share of your 215 in-inbox engagements (18 direct + 197 team) that were shown in NOW or TODAY before you engaged.
- **V1** (the shipped rules) was replayed on 2026-10-26 by wrapping `sim.py`. Its definition is in [research/backtest/README.md](../research/backtest/README.md).

### The baseline gate

The earlier plan said: beat "direct ask or usual author" by +10pp, or stop. That gate would fire, because the usual-author rule alone (any author you engaged with in 28 days) already reaches 0.78 at 26.3 cards/wk. The author prior _is_ that baseline, and it ships as rules. V1 matches its recall (0.80 vs 0.78) with 15% fewer cards (22.3 vs 26.3) and ~1.5 fewer on screen. What the desk adds beyond the baseline is that lower load, protected asks, your PRs and repos, Audit, and handoffs. So v1 is gated on the golden test against the V1 replay, then on week-2 use.

### Limitations

- Labels count only visible reviews and comments. Slack approvals, silent reads and PRs you should have reviewed are invisible, so the replay measures agreement with your habits, which your tooling already shaped (seer/sentry notifications on Ignore since 2026-06-27).
- A teammate's review that satisfies a team request leaves no removal event, so team items stay "active" longer than they really were.
- CI, mergeability and Stale labels can't be reconstructed, so every PR was treated as green and mergeable (27% were failing CI in the dry run). Live `blocked` therefore differs from the replay.
- CODEOWNERS uses half-monthly snapshots. Size and file lists are final-state values; only the first 100 files are used.
- Affinity uses your merged getsentry PRs as a proxy for commit history.
- 85 population PRs had >100 timeline events, so late re-requests on huge fan-out PRs could be missed.
- Cards are a heuristic (same author, same scope, 72h). The replay doesn't model your done, snooze or mute actions. Caps apply to all cards, including asks, which live are cap-exempt (about 1.4 asks a week).
- Right-censoring: the last two weeks had less time to be engaged. Excluding requests after 10-12 moves the base rate from 5.6% to 5.5%.
- The topic-keyword variant leaks future information.
- One author dominates. The prior can feed on itself.
- Only the focus repos are covered. 40 unasked engagements in OSS and tool repos (dotagents, warden, skillet, junior-prod, toolkit, .github) are outside the model. Your own PRs are outside it too, so MINE volume is unmeasured.
- Time to engage is wall-clock, with no adjustment for hours, time zones or PTO. The direct sample is small (n=18).

## 5. Landscape

- **GitHub's /pulls dashboard** (GA 2026-07-09) has inbox sections, saved views and new qualifiers, but no API and no path filter. (github.blog changelog 2026-07-09)
- **gh-dash 4.26:** YAML search sections plus shell keybindings. About 40% of the desk in an afternoon, as a stopgap. (gh-dash docs)
- **CodeRabbit Triage** (beta): deterministic workflow state, but it excludes team requests and can't match paths or CODEOWNERS. (coderabbit.ai blog and docs)
- **Graphite, Octobox, Linear, Reviewable, Aviator:** sections, done-until-new-activity, snooze until time or activity, whose-turn lists. (their docs)
- **T3 Code:** a control surface over several agents. Its MCP server is read-only by default, and its MCP Apps render only in Codex threads from 0.1.0. You have 0.0.40. (github.com/pingdotgg/t3code docs)
- **Codex:** app-server JSON-RPC; `codex mcp-server` was removed. (learn.chatgpt.com)
- **Churn:** Vibe Kanban is sunsetting, Terragon shut down, Crystal became Nimbalyst, Superset became Paradev. (`gh repo view`)
- **Gap:** nothing found ranks team requests using local signals such as author history, affinity or your open PRs.
- **Patterns borrowed:** whose-turn plus reason codes, deterministic first, done-until-new-activity, snooze-until-event, a hard cap on the top bucket, and agent sessions as attention items.

## 6. Design lineage

- Three proposals were judged by three judges: evidence-first (total 107), standalone-first (98) and embedded-first (87). Each won one judge's lens.
- design_v1 = evidence-first plus grafts: the in-Desktop surface, the host spike, level-triggered snoozes (from embedded-first); the Launcher/Tracker/Runner split, review rooms, series grouping, the read-only GitHub type (from standalone-first).
- Three reviews of design_v1 produced this v2:
  - **Red-team:** read confinement for rooms, `git archive` attribute and symlink handling, profiles for untrusted repos, draft laundering, topic matching on PR text, and the spike's `$.prompt.submit`.
  - **Host feasibility:** the `start_session` model cap and wake rule, SendMessage in rooms, `--restricted` not being tool-less, MCP profile selection, keyboard limits, Drizzle, CEL, `osascript`, `isRequired` batching.
  - **Scope fit:** the four deviations, missing repo management, an overbuilt M1, the rule-set mismatch with P9, busy-week survival, and cutting the web page.
